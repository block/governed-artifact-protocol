<DomainNav domain="research" />

Publish a fictional paper asking whether floating gummy worms remember stories better. Release the two earlier publications it builds on, a pace study and a recall-quiz methods paper, then save a submitted manuscript, record peer feedback, and revise its conclusion. Correct a source while the manuscript is still a draft, then have an editor approve the final version and a separate publisher authorize its publication. Independently verify the resulting evidence.

Every research group and result is invented. This is a local, single-user demonstration with public demonstration signing keys, JSON storage, and no connection to an actual journal. The server exposes tools rather than a graphical document editor. [Set up the demo](../run-the-demos.md) once and read the [modeling guide](../research.mdx) for the profiles and roles.

## Before you start

The application is Research journal, with ID `research`. `get_research_workflow` returns four signed, pre-ratified models and configured participants. You select a role in conversation; the demo does not authenticate your identity. Ordinary startup preserves existing work. Use the reset at the end only when you want to discard this application's local changes.

The client keeps IDs, digests, source entries, and preview confirmations. You review content in ordinary language; you do not need to transcribe machine values. When the agent saves a complete manuscript, it must preserve all fields and the exact sources actually used. See [the tool reference](../how-the-demos-work.mdx#research) for input schemas.

## 1. Release the source material

<Steps block ids="create-version authorize-release release" />

Two fictional, previously published papers will appear in the new paper's bibliography. Each answers a different question from the new paper: the study measured story pace, and the methods paper defines a recall test. Their required payload fields are `kind`, `title`, `authors`, `journal`, `year`, and `body`. The demo releases local representations of these publications so the new manuscript can pin their exact versions.

<Chat>

Body: “Aboard the spaceship Sugar Comet, twenty fictional groups of gummy worms told the same tale of a marshmallow moon, half with gravity switched on and half floating. Groups with gravity finished in an average of 60 seconds; floating groups finished in 40 seconds. Floating storytellers paused less because nobody had to climb back onto the story rug. The study measured pace only; it did not test what listeners remembered. All results are invented demonstration data.”
</You>
<You>
Switch to the cited-works editor and preview that exact study for release.
</You>
<Agent>
The study reports invented story pace only: 60 seconds with gravity and 40 seconds floating. Releasing this version will make its exact source proof available. The cited-works editor is the only participant configured to release sources.
</Agent>
<You>
Release that study version.
</You>
<You>

Its kind is methods-paper, title is “The Jellybean Quiz: ten questions for testing what a listener remembers of any story,” and credited authors are “Fictional Story Metrics Group.” Journal is “Fictional Journal of Story Metrics” and publication year is 2023. <Expect>
`verify_research_proof` checks the pinned JSON Schema contract, profile and payload digests, exact decision binding, and all carried authority signatures. A paper checked against the cited-works store, or a source against the journal store, fails because that store does not trust its releaser. MCP wraps array results as `items`; pass one entry as the proof, not the entire wrapper. `sweep_research_sources` reports aligned references to the corrected study, methods paper, peer report, and editor notes. ## Tools and interpretation

The cited-works editor releases studies and methods papers. The peer saves reports and the editor saves notes, under distinct profiles; the editor also approves or rejects manuscripts. The publisher releases reports, notes, and approved papers. These are local permissions and content policies. Profiles and authority decisions are signed with public demonstration keys held by the application. Consumers configure trust separately from the proof. Section anchors, bibliography labels, and review responses are domain fields rather than new GAP record types.

This walkthrough keeps one review report. It does not model comment movement, review rounds, external source retrieval, or the evidentiary meaning of peer review. Publication and verification do not establish scientific correctness. The [modeling guide](../research.mdx) explains these limits and the [open questions](https://github.com/block/governed-artifact-protocol/blob/main/DEFERRED.md) preserve protocol questions separately from the demo's local choices.
